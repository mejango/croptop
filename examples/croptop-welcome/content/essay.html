Croptop TLDR

Self-serve websites with content feeds and revenue streams baked in.

You’re looking at it now. This site is a Croptop site.

The writing, the music, the little instruments, the garden: they are posts. They live together because a website can hold more than one kind of thought. You choose the form that fits the work.

A place you can keep

Most publishing starts with an account. Your work enters someone else’s system, takes the shape that system permits, and reaches the people its feed decides to show it to. That can be useful. It also puts a changing set of rules between you and your own archive.

Croptop starts with a site on your computer. A small app makes posting easy and connects your work to peers. The files are yours to inspect, back up, move, and change. Your publishing key lets you keep the same site when you move to another machine.

Make the post fit the idea

Write a page. Upload a recording. Show a film. When an idea needs interaction, give it code. The rhythm machine on this site is a post you can play; the garden is a post that changes while you watch.

You can begin with the template and make it your own as you go. A website already has room for these things. Croptop makes that room easy to use.

Let readers help it last

Publishing adds your site to IPFS, where content can travel between peers. People who follow your site in Croptop keep copies and help serve them. Your readers can take part in keeping the work available.

Copies still need someone to hold them. A site stays reachable while peers or hosts keep serving it. The point is that this work can be shared, and the original publisher does not have to be its only keeper.

A stable address points readers toward the current version. Edit and publish again when the work changes. Older versions may remain available; your site has somewhere to continue.

Support can belong here too

To sell posts, you can make a shop that connects to your site. Readers can support your work with a few clicks. Buying requires a wallet, posting and reading remain available without one.

Start small

You do not need a finished identity or a content strategy. Make a site. Add a post. Publish to peers.

Then keep going. The next thing you make already has a place.
